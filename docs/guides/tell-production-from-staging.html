How to tell staging from production in your browser

Updated November 9, 2026

Almost every team has a story about it: someone ran a migration, deleted a record or refunded an order, and only afterwards noticed the tab was production. The cause is rarely carelessness. It's that staging and production are built to be identical, so the one thing that tells them apart is a hostname in the address bar that you stopped reading weeks ago.

This guide covers the common ways to make the environment obvious, what each is good at, and how to set up color-coded tabs in about two minutes.

Why the mistake is so easy to make

  • Environments run the same code, so the pages look and behave the same.
  • The only difference is a URL fragment such as staging., -dev or a port number, and the eye skips it.
  • You usually have several tabs open for several environments at once, and you switch between them quickly.
  • The cost is lopsided: a wrong click in staging is free, in production it isn't.

Ways to make the environment obvious

1. A banner or ribbon in your own app

Many teams add a "STAGING" ribbon to non-production builds. It's effective and costs nothing at runtime, but it only covers apps you control, and it has to be built and kept up to date in every one of them. It does nothing for a vendor dashboard, a cloud console or a third-party admin panel.

2. Favicon badges and tab-title prefixes

Prefixing the title with [STG] or swapping the favicon helps when you scan the tab strip. They are small, though, and with twenty tabs open they blend in. They also disappear from view once you're focused on the page itself.

3. Separate browser profiles

Keeping production in its own profile or window is a strong separation. It also means separate logins, extensions and bookmarks, which people tend to abandon after a week.

4. Color-code the page itself

A colored border or tint around the whole page sits in your peripheral vision the entire time you work, whatever site you're on and whether or not you own it. This is what URLColors does, and it's the lowest-effort option that covers third-party sites.

Set up color-coded environments in two minutes

  1. Install URLColors from the Chrome Web Store, Edge Add-ons or Firefox Add-ons, and pin it to your toolbar.
  2. Open the popup and add one rule per line: a piece of the URL, then a color.
  3. Open an environment. The matching tabs get a border straight away.

Example rules:

production.myapp.com, red
staging.myapp.com, orange
localhost:3000, green
*.preview.myapp.com, purple

Tips for a scheme that holds up

  • Use the same colors everywhere. Red for production, orange or yellow for staging and UAT, green for local. Consistency is what makes it automatic.
  • Save the loudest style for production. Add flashing or a thicker border to the one environment where a mistake costs money.
  • Match what actually differs. A keyword matches anywhere in the URL, so you can match a subdomain, a port, or a path like /admin.
  • Use wildcards for preview deploys. One *.preview.myapp.com rule covers every branch URL.
  • Snooze before you share your screen. A few minutes of snooze gives you a clean screenshot or demo, and the borders come back on their own.

Frequently asked

Does this stop me from clicking in production? No. It makes the environment impossible to miss, which is what prevents most mistakes. It doesn't block or confirm actions.

Does it send my browsing data anywhere? No. Your rules stay in your browser and the extension makes no network requests. Read the privacy policy or the source code.
